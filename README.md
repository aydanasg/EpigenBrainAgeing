# $\color{#C71585}{\textsf{Oligodendrocytes: Epigenomic Remodelling Across Lifespan}}$

## Abstract

Human brain ageing is a leading risk factor for AD and SVD. Loss of white matter volume, strongly associated with ageing, is also a clinical marker of AD and SVD. Oligodendrocytes myelinate axons in the brain and are the primary contributors to white matter. Age-related changes in oligodendrocyte lineage cells include altered myelination-associated gene expression and reduced OPC populations. Ageing is also accompanied by changes in chromatin accessibility, three-dimensional genome organisation, and DNA methylation. However, how the oligodendrocyte active regulome changes across the lifespan remains unknown. This study profiles the H3K27ac landscape in OLIG2<sup>+</sup> nuclei from 47 donors spanning fetal development to late adulthood. The most pronounced regulome differences occur in the fetal and late-adulthood stages. Regions of hypoacetylation in late adulthood in oligodendrocytes involve genes implicated in epigenetic and chromatin regulation and genome stability. Oligodendrocyte regulatory regions show enrichment for SCZ, SVD-WMH and PD heritability at all stages, whereas AD heritability enrichment emerges in adulthood. Integration with chromatin interactions shows that putative risk genes are stage-dependent, with the AD risk genes *EED*, *BIN1* and *PICALM* increasing in significance from foetal development to late adulthood. Analysis of linear and non-linear changes identified a trajectory enriched for OPC markers that lose H3K27ac across the lifespan, consistent with a decline in the proportion of OPCs with age. AD-associated regulatory changes were largely distinct from age-associated changes, and where they overlapped, did not simply accelerate ageing.
